## Title
Your Bones Make 2–3 Million Blood Cells Every Second

## Description
Right now, the red marrow inside your bones is making 2 to 3 million new red blood cells every second. It never stops, because you carry about 25 trillion red cells and each one wears out after about 120 days. In the same second, your heart beats about once and pushes out about 70 milliliters of blood, and your fastest nerve signals hit 120 meters per second, fast enough to cross a soccer field in under a second. Add up all the cells your body replaces and it comes to about 330 billion a day, almost 4 million every second.

How many times will your heart beat in a lifetime? About 3 million, or about 3 billion? Tell me in the comments.

## Real answers (for reference, not for posting)
B, about 3 billion (roughly 2.5-3 billion; our arithmetic, not a quoted figure). At a typical resting ~70 beats per minute: 70 × 60 × 24 × 365.25 ≈ 36.8 million beats a year; over ~80 years that is ≈ 2.9 billion (American Heart Association resting range 60-100 bpm; WHO life expectancy ≈ 73 years worldwide, ≈ 80 in high-income countries; 60-80 bpm over 73-80 years gives ≈ 2.3-3.4 billion). A) 3 million is only about one month of heartbeats (70 × 60 × 24 × 30 ≈ 3.0 million). Cross-check with the film: "your heart beats about once" a second, and a lifetime is about 2.5 billion seconds. The answer never appears on screen. Levine 1997 is context only: its abstract gives ≈ 7.3 × 10^8 beats per lifetime for mammals in general, not the human figure.
