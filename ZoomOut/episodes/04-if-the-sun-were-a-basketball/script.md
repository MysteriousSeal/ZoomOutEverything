# 04 — If the Sun Were a Basketball (60 s)

- **Anchor:** a basketball (24 cm)
- **Ladder:** Mercury → Venus → Earth + Moon → Mars → Jupiter → Saturn → Uranus + Neptune → the nearest star
- **Reveal:** the nearest star would be a ping-pong ball as far away as New York is from Rome
- **Length:** ~60 s (13 lines, 131 words)
- **Accent colour:** solar orange `(255, 160, 70)`
- **Production:** ✅ rendered: `out/04-if-the-sun-were-a-basketball.mp4` (59.7 s, −14 LUFS) + `out/cover.png` (2.5 s, headline + basketball Sun). See `BRIEF.md`, `episode.json`, `camera.js`, `env.js`, `scenes/`; output in `out/`
- **Signature transition:** one continuous top-down pull-back. The orbits draw themselves as concentric circles around the basketball Sun, the ground turns from a plaza to a park to a town, and the final zoom lifts off the map onto a globe with a flight arc from New York to Rome.

## Narration script

> Shrink the Sun down to the size of a basketball.
> Mercury becomes a grain of sand, ten meters away.
> Venus, a peppercorn, about nineteen meters out.
> Earth is a peppercorn too, twenty-six meters away.
> And the Moon? A speck of dust, a hand's width from Earth.
> Mars, a mustard seed, thirty-nine meters out.
> Jupiter, a grape, a hundred and thirty meters away.
> Saturn, a smaller grape, two hundred and fifty meters out.
> Uranus and Neptune, two coffee beans, | half a kilometer and nearly eight hundred meters away.
> That's the whole solar system: | a basketball, a few crumbs, and a lot of nothing.
> And the nearest star? | Another ball, a ping-pong ball this time…
> as far away as New York is from Rome.
> Space isn't crowded. | It's almost entirely empty.
