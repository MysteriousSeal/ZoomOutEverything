## Title
What Happens on the Internet in One Second?

## Description
More than 99% of the internet between continents runs under the sea, through cables that in the deep sea are often about as thick as a garden hose. In one second people type more than 100,000 searches into Google and send about 4 million emails, and data centers use roughly 1.5% of the world's electricity. One of these numbers is stranger than the rest.

What damages undersea internet cables the most? Shark bites, or fishing gear and ship anchors? Tell me in the comments.

## Real answers (for reference, not for posting)
B, fishing gear and ship anchors, by far. The International Cable Protection Committee (ICPC) says about 70-80% of cable incidents come from accidental damage by commercial fishing and ship anchors (TeleGeography: "two-thirds of all cable faults"); dragged anchors alone cause about 30% of incidents, around 60 faults a year, out of roughly 150-200 faults worldwide each year. Most of the rest come from abrasion, equipment failure and natural hazards (earthquakes, underwater landslides, currents). A) Shark and fish bites caused under 1% of faults up to 2006 (about 0.5% in 1959-2006), and none were recorded from 2007 to 2014. The answer never appears in the film.
