## Title
Without the Moon, Moonlit Nights Would Be 100× Darker

## Description
The Moon is already drifting away from Earth, a little farther every single year. But what if it disappeared all at once? Moonlit nights about 100 times darker, tides a third of today's biggest and no more total solar eclipses. Without the Moon, many models say Earth's tilt could also wobble far more, and the seasons could go haywire.

How long until total solar eclipses end? About 6,000 years, or about 600 million years? Tell me in the comments.

## Real answers (for reference, not for posting)
B. In roughly 600 million years (the commonly cited estimate; it is approximate) the receding Moon's disc will look too small to cover the Sun completely, so total solar eclipses will end and only annular ones will remain. At about 3.8 cm a year, 6,000 years moves the Moon only about 230 m, far too little to matter.
