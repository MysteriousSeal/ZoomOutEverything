## Narration script

> How long is a million seconds?
> About eleven and a half days. A short vacation.
> A billion seconds is almost thirty-two years.
> That's the difference between a million and a billion.
> A trillion seconds ago, mammoths still roamed the Earth, and humans were painting caves.
> That's thirty-one thousand, seven hundred years.
> And counting the US national debt, one dollar per second, would take over a million years.

## Fact-check notes

- 10⁶ s ÷ 86,400 ≈ **11.57 days**.
- 10⁹ s ÷ 31,557,600 (Julian year) ≈ **31.7 years**.
- 10¹² s ≈ **31,700 years**.
- ~31,700 years ago: woolly mammoths were widespread (they died out ~4,000 years ago), and Upper Paleolithic cave art existed (e.g. Chauvet Cave, ~30,000–36,000 years old).
- **US national debt:** over $37 trillion in late 2025 and rising. Check the current figure (TreasuryDirect "Debt to the Penny") before publishing. Any debt above $31.7 trillion counted at $1 per second takes > 1,000,000 years (e.g. $37T ÷ 31.56M s/yr ≈ 1.17M years), so the claim holds as long as the debt stays above ~$31.6T.
