## Description
Crust, mantle, a sea of liquid iron, then straight through the center of the planet and out the other side. In this thought experiment you fall through the planet at up to about 8 km/s (5 mi/s), with no air to slow you. In the simplest model the whole trip takes the same time between any two points on Earth, and the number may surprise you. (The film uses the classic uniform-density model. Earth's real interior is denser toward the core, which makes the straight drop a few minutes faster.)

Through the Moon instead, would the trip be shorter or longer? Tell me in the comments.

## Real answers (for reference, not for posting)
B, longer: about 54 minutes one way, in the same uniform-density model the film uses. The one-way time is π·√(R/g) = π·√(3/(4πGρ)), so it depends only on the density ρ, not on the size of the planet. The Moon (3.34 g/cm³) is less dense than Earth (5.51 g/cm³), so the trip takes √(5.51/3.34) ≈ 1.28 times as long: 42.2 min × 1.28 ≈ 54 min. (NASA Moon Fact Sheet, mean density 3,344 kg/m³: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/moonfact.html; Earth Fact Sheet, 5,513 kg/m³: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html)
