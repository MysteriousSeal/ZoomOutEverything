## Fact-check notes

- **A day = one spin:** a solar day is 24 h by definition. The sidereal rotation is 23 h 56 min; the narration says "one spin" and that is fine at this level (the solar day is one spin relative to the Sun).
- **Month ≈ 29.5 days:** the mean synodic month (new moon to new moon) is 29.530589 days (NASA / Meeus, *Astronomical Algorithms*). "Roughly", because calendar months are 28–31 days.
- **Year = one orbit:** the tropical year is 365.2422 days, shown as 365¼ (the Julian approximation).
- **Nothing takes a week:** no astronomical cycle visible to the naked eye has a 7-day period. The quarter-month is the closest.
- **A lunar phase ≈ 7.4 days:** 29.53 / 4 = 7.38 days. This is the "lunar quarter" hypothesis, which is commonly cited but not proven. Hence "One theory". Babylonian *hemerologies* marked the 7th, 14th, 19th, 21st and 28th days of the lunar month as special (Assyrian/Neo-Babylonian, 7th c. BC). Sources: Encyclopaedia Britannica, "week"; E. G. Richards, *Mapping Time* (OUP 1998); Zerubavel, *The Seven Day Circle* (1985).
- **Babylon, seven moving lights:** Mesopotamian astronomers tracked the seven naked-eye "wanderers" (Sun, Moon and five planets) long before the Greeks. Uranus is too faint to have been recognised as a planet without a telescope (found 1781). "Historians trace" is a hedge: the link between the seven planets and the seven-day week is a mainstream view, but the week's exact origin is debated.
- **Planets = "wanderers":** from Greek *planētēs* (πλανήτης), "wanderer" (Oxford English Dictionary).
- **Seven as a sacred number:** 7 is prominent in Mesopotamian ritual and texts (the *Sebettu*, the seven gods) and in the Hebrew Bible. The claim is deliberately general.
- **Jewish week, Sabbath:** the six-days-of-work-and-a-Sabbath cycle (Exodus 20:8–11; Genesis 2:2–3) runs continuously, independent of the Moon. This is why it is considered the direct ancestor of the continuous modern week. The era chip says "c. 600 BC · JUDAH" (the Kingdom of Judah until 587 BC). The Sabbath is attested in pre-exilic prophets (Amos 8:5).
- **Christianity and Islam spread it:** Constantine made Sunday a day of rest in AD 321. Islam keeps the 7-day week with Friday (*jumuʿa*) prayer from the 7th century. The era chip uses "c. AD 600".
- **Roman astrologers / planetary days:** the planetary week (each day ruled by one of the seven planets, in the order Saturn, Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, from the "planetary hours" scheme) is Hellenistic-Roman. It is attested by the 1st century AD (Pompeii graffiti; Cassius Dio, *Roman History* 37.18–19, c. AD 200, explains it). The era chip says "c. AD 100".
- **Saturday/Sunday/Monday:** Old English *Sæterndæg* (Saturn's day), *Sunnandæg*, *Mōnandæg*, calques of Latin *dies Saturni, dies Solis, dies Lunae*.
- **French:** mardi < *Martis dies*, mercredi < *Mercurii dies*, jeudi < *Jovis dies* (Jupiter), vendredi < *Veneris dies*. (Samedi comes from *sambati dies*, the Sabbath, and dimanche from *dies dominica*, the Lord's day. That is why the script only claims "the rest", i.e. Tuesday to Friday. Lundi = the Moon's day, consistent.)
- **English Norse gods:** Tuesday = Tīw (Norse Týr), Wednesday = Woden (Odin), Thursday = Thor (Þunor), Friday = Frigg (some argue Freyja; Frigg is the standard etymology, OED). These are Germanic gods; "Norse" is the common popular label (Old English used the West Germanic forms). The on-screen text uses TYR, WODEN, THOR, FRIGG.
- **French Republican calendar:** adopted by decree on 24 Oct 1793 (dated retroactively from 22 Sep 1792). Its 10-day *décade* ended with *décadi* as the rest day. Napoleon's decree of 9 Sep 1805 abolished it from 1 Jan 1806, so it lasted about 12 years (1793–1805). The décade had already weakened after the 1802 Concordat restored Sunday.
- **Soviet weeks:** the *nepreryvka* (continuous 5-day week) began in Oct 1929. A fixed 6-day week (rest on the 6th, 12th, 18th, 24th and 30th) followed in Dec 1931. The 7-day week returned by decree on 26 June 1940. So: "five- and six-day weeks", "by 1940, seven was back".
- **Scale note:** the orrery in beats 2–6 is a diagram, not to scale, and is never presented as scale.
