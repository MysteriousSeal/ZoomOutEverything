## Title
The Moon Is Already Leaving Us. What If It Vanished?

## Description
The Moon is already drifting away from Earth, a little farther every single year. But what if it disappeared all at once? Nights about 100 times darker, tides a third of today's biggest and no more total solar eclipses. Many models also say Earth's tilt could wobble far more over millions of years, and seasons could go haywire.

How long until the Moon is too far away for total solar eclipses? About 6,000 years, or about 600 million years? Tell me in the comments.

## Real answers (for reference, not for posting)
B. In roughly 600 million years (the commonly cited estimate; it is approximate) the receding Moon's disc will look too small to cover the Sun completely, so total solar eclipses will end and only annular ones will remain. At about 3.8 cm a year, 6,000 years moves the Moon only about 230 m, far too little to matter.
