6. Where do you sit when you fly? | Tell me in the comments.

## FACT-CHECK

- **Flying is very safe:** fatal accidents on commercial jets are rare (Boeing Statistical Summary 2025: worldwide fleet). Don't scare people off flying; the tone is "here's how to be ready".
- **Most crashes are survivable:** the NTSB's review of US airline accidents 1983–2000 found 95.7% of occupants survived (widely cited; even in serious accidents with fire, a majority survived). Say "most plane crashes are survivable", no exact % on screen unless attributed ("NTSB, US 1983–2000").
- **Phase of flight (Boeing 2025 Statistical Summary, worldwide commercial jets, fatal accidents 2016–2025, 31 fatal accidents):** takeoff 16%, initial climb 6% (together 22%); final approach 13%, landing 35%; total ≈ 70%. These phases are ≈ 1% + 1% + 3% + 1% ≈ 6% of flight time for a typical 1.5-hour flight. Cruise is 57% of flight time and 10% of fatal accidents. On screen: "≈ 70% OF FATAL ACCIDENTS · ≈ 6% OF FLIGHT TIME · BOEING, 2016–2025".
- **90 seconds:** FAA/EASA certification requires that a full aircraft can be evacuated within 90 seconds using only half the exits. Fire and smoke can spread through a cabin in minutes.
- **Count the rows:** standard cabin-safety advice (FAA, flight attendants): know the number of rows to your nearest exit (front and behind); in smoke or darkness you may need to feel your way along the seat backs. Floor-path lighting helps.
- **Seatbelt:** low and tight across the hips (FAA).
- **Shoes on:** keep sturdy shoes on during takeoff and landing; you may have to walk over debris or hot surfaces (FAA advice includes wearing shoes, removing high heels for the slide).
- **Brace position:** FAA brace position: bend forward, head down against the seat back in front (or as low as possible), feet flat on the floor, slightly behind the knees. Shown as a refined safety-card pictogram.
- **Leave your bag:** FAA: leave all carry-on baggage behind. Passengers retrieving bags slowed evacuations in documented accidents (e.g. Aeroflot Flight 1492, Moscow 2019; Emirates 521, Dubai 2016; Japan Airlines 516, Tokyo 2024 where everyone left without bags and all 379 survived). "Grabbing luggage has cost lives" is supported by investigators' and safety experts' assessments (e.g. Aeroflot 1492); don't name an airline on screen.
- Avoid: airline names/logos, real crash footage or gore, claiming a "safest seat" as fact.
