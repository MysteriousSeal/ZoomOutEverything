## Title
How Small Is an Atom? Smaller Than You Can Imagine

## Description
Start with a human hair, about 70 micrometers wide, and zoom in past blood cells, bacteria, viruses and DNA until you reach a single atom. Then comes the part that makes no sense.

Does knowing you're mostly empty space change how you see yourself? Tell me in the comments.

Every video starts close and zooms out. Subscribe for a new one every day.

Sources:
Typical human hair diameter, about 17 to 180 micrometers (about 70 typical)
Red blood cell diameter, about 7.5 to 8 micrometers
E. coli bacterium width, about 1 micrometer; influenza virus, about 80 to 120 nm
DNA double helix width, about 2 nm
Atomic radii data: hydrogen about 0.1 nm, carbon about 0.14 to 0.17 nm
Nucleus diameter about 1/10,000 to 1/100,000 of the atom's (a hydrogen nucleus is about 1.7 femtometers)
'Empty' is a simplification: electrons aren't tiny balls but a spread-out cloud of probability
Half a million assumes a carbon-sized atom (~0.14 nm); smaller atoms like hydrogen (~0.1 nm) give ~700,000
