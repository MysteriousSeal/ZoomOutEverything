## Narration script

> How deep is the ocean, really?
> At two hundred meters, sunlight is basically gone.
> At three hundred thirty-two: the deepest scuba dive ever.
> Sink the Burj Khalifa here, and it vanishes completely.
> At three thousand meters, a whale is still hunting, on a single breath.
> At three thousand eight hundred, the Titanic.
> And at eleven thousand, the bottom. *(0.9 s pause)* Drop Everest in, and there's still two kilometers of water above it.

## Fact-check notes

- **Photic zone:** sunlight sufficient for photosynthesis ends at ~200 m.
- **Scuba record:** 332.35 m, Ahmed Gabr (2014, Guinness). Check whether it has been broken before publishing.
- **Burj Khalifa:** 828 m tall.
- **Cuvier's beaked whale:** deepest recorded mammal dive 2,992 m (Quick et al., 2020), on a single breath.
- **Titanic wreck:** ~3,800 m (12,500 ft).
- **Challenger Deep:** ~10,935 m (estimates range from ~10,920 to 10,994 m).
- **Everest:** 8,849 m, and 10,935 − 8,849 ≈ 2,086 m, hence "two kilometers of water above it".
- **Pressure at the bottom:** ~1,086 bar ≈ 1.1 tonnes-force per cm² (≈ 15,750 psi).
- Imperial conversions use 1 m = 3.281 ft, rounded.
