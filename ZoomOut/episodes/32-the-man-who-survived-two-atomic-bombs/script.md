## FACT-CHECK

- **Tsutomu Yamaguchi** (16 March 1916 – 4 January 2010), naval engineer for Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, from Nagasaki; in Hiroshima on a three-month business trip, due to leave on 6 August 1945.
- **Hiroshima, 6 August 1945, 8:15 a.m.** ("Little Boy"): he was about 3 km from ground zero; burns to the upper left side of his body, ruptured eardrums, temporarily blinded. He spent the night in an air-raid shelter.
- **7 August:** took a train home to Nagasaki (arrived the 8th), treated, bandaged.
- **Nagasaki, 9 August 1945, 11:02 a.m.** ("Fat Man"): back at work in the Mitsubishi office, he was describing the Hiroshima blast to his supervisor when the flash came, again about 3 km from ground zero. He survived again.
- **Recognition:** recognised as a hibakusha of Nagasaki in 1957; the Japanese government officially recognised his presence at Hiroshima on 24 March 2009, making him the only person officially recognised as a "nijū hibakusha" (survivor of both bombings). More than 160 people experienced both.
- **Died** of stomach cancer on 4 January 2010, aged 93.
