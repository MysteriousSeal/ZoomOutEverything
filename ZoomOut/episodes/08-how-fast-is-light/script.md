## Narration script

> How fast is light, really?
> Three hundred thousand kilometers, every single second.
> In one second, light could circle the Earth seven and a half times.
> It reaches the Moon in just over a second.
> Sunlight takes about eight minutes to get here.
> So when you look at the Sun, | you're seeing it as it was eight minutes ago.
> Light from Neptune takes about four hours.
> Voyager 1, our most distant spacecraft, | is almost a full day away at light speed.
> The nearest star? | Its light travels for more than four years to reach us.
> From the far side of our galaxy, | the light has been traveling for tens of thousands of years.
> And the most distant galaxies we've seen? | Their light set off more than thirteen billion years ago.
> Light is the fastest thing in the universe.
> And the universe is still too big for it.

## Fact-check notes

- c = 299,792.458 km/s.
- Earth's equatorial circumference 40,075 km → 299,792 / 40,075 ≈ 7.48 laps per second.
- Earth–Moon 384,400 km → 1.28 s.
- Earth–Sun 149.6 M km → 499 s ≈ 8 min 19 s.
- Earth–Neptune ≈ 4.3–4.7 B km depending on positions → ≈ 4–4.3 h (Sun–Neptune 4.17 h).
- Voyager 1 distance ≈ 25 B km in late 2026 → ≈ 23 light-hours ("almost a full day"). It passes 1 light-day around late 2026/2027, so check before publishing.
- Proxima Centauri 4.24 ly.
- The far side of the Milky Way is ~70,000–80,000 ly from us (galaxy ~100,000 ly across, Sun ~26,000 ly from the centre).
- JWST galaxies such as JADES-GS-z14-0 have light-travel times ≈ 13.5 billion years.
