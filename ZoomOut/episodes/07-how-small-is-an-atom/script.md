## Narration script

> How small is an atom, really?
> Start with a single human hair, about seventy micrometers wide.
> Ten red blood cells, side by side, would just span it.
> Each of those cells is about seven times wider than a bacterium.
> A virus is roughly ten times smaller still.
> Inside your cells, DNA is a strand just two nanometers wide.
> And an atom? | About a tenth of a nanometer.
> It would take half a million atoms, side by side, | to cross that one hair.
> But here's the strange part: | an atom is almost entirely empty.
> Blow one up to the size of a football stadium, | and its nucleus would be a grain of sand on the center spot.
> The electrons? | Just a faint haze, out in the stands.
> Everything in between is empty space.
> Including the atoms that make up you.

## Fact-check notes

- Human hair diameter ~17–180 µm; ~70 µm is typical.
- Red blood cell ~7.5–8 µm diameter, so ~9–10 across a 70 µm hair.
- E. coli ~1 µm wide (2 µm long); 7 µm / 1 µm ≈ 7.
- Influenza virus ~80–120 nm, so ~10× smaller than a 1 µm bacterium.
- DNA double helix ≈ 2 nm wide.
- Atom diameters ~0.1–0.5 nm (carbon ≈ 0.14–0.17 nm; hydrogen ≈ 0.1 nm). "About a tenth of a nanometer" fits small atoms.
- 70 µm ÷ 0.14 nm ≈ 500,000.
- Nucleus ≈ 1/10,000–1/100,000 of the atom's diameter. Atom → 100–150 m stadium ⇒ nucleus ≈ 1–15 mm. "A grain of sand" is at the small end (hydrogen-like) and a common ELI5 framing; a marble is also used. Note it in a pinned reply if challenged.
- "Empty space" is a simplification: electrons are spread out as probability clouds rather than tiny balls. Hence "a faint haze".
- Pinned comment: the oft-cited "humanity would fit in a sugar cube" estimate follows from nuclear density (~2.3 × 10¹⁷ kg/m³): 5 × 10¹¹ kg ÷ 2.3 × 10¹⁷ ≈ 2 cm³.
