# Zoom Out: The Heaviest Spoon in the Universe

**Series:** True Scale · **Length:** ≈ 52.8 s · **Voice:** ElevenLabs Brittney, Eleven v4 · **Hook:** shocking claim, readable on frame 1 · **Ending:** comments question

## Narration ("|" = phrase pause)

0. One teaspoon of this weighs more than every human on Earth. | Combined.
1. It's a neutron star: | what's left when a giant star dies and its core collapses.
2. More mass than our Sun, | crushed into a ball twenty kilometres wide: the size of a city.
3. Atoms are crushed so hard | that protons and electrons merge into neutrons, packed shoulder to shoulder.
4. So one teaspoon weighs about a billion tonnes. | All eight billion of us together weigh less.
5. And some of them spin over seven hundred times a second.
6. Drop a marshmallow on one, | and it hits with the energy of an atomic bomb.
7. What's the wildest space fact you know? | Tell me in the comments.

## FACT-CHECK

- **Neutron star:** the collapsed core left after a massive star (≈ 8–25 solar masses) explodes as a supernova. Typical mass ≈ 1.4 solar masses (range ≈ 1.1–2.3), radius ≈ 10–12 km, so ≈ 20–24 km across ("twenty kilometres wide": OK).
- **Density:** ≈ 4 × 10¹⁷ kg/m³ (nuclear density ≈ 2.3 × 10¹⁷, higher in the core). A teaspoon (5 mL = 5 × 10⁻⁶ m³) ≈ 1–3 × 10¹² kg = **about 1–3 billion tonnes**. Say "about a billion tonnes" (conservative). Never compare with Everest (≈ 160 billion tonnes: heavier than the spoon).
- **Humanity:** ≈ 8.2 billion people × ≈ 50–62 kg average (adults and children) ≈ **0.4–0.5 billion tonnes**. So the teaspoon outweighs all humans combined (by ≈ 2–6×).
- **Neutrons:** under the pressure, electrons and protons combine into neutrons (inverse beta decay / electron capture); matter is mostly neutrons packed at nuclear density.
- **Spin:** the fastest known pulsar, PSR J1748−2446ad, spins 716 times per second.
- **Marshmallow:** surface escape (impact) speed for 1.4 M☉ and R = 10–12 km is √(r_s/R)·c ≈ 0.59–0.64 c (≈ 180,000–190,000 km/s); a 7 g marshmallow then carries (γ−1)mc² ≈ 1.2–1.9 × 10¹⁴ J ≈ 30–45 kilotons of TNT (≈ 20 kt with the simpler ½mv² at 0.5 c). Hiroshima ≈ 15 kt. On screen: "≈ 60% OF THE SPEED OF LIGHT" and "≈ 20–40 KILOTONS · HIROSHIMA ≈ 15 KT". "The energy of an atomic bomb": OK (understated, never overstated).
- Visual notes: the teaspoon must look like a real steel teaspoon; the neutron star a believable dense glowing sphere with magnetic-field lines / pulsar beams (stylised but not cartoon); "20 km = a city": compare with a real city outline at true scale (e.g. a generic city grid, or Manhattan-sized without naming, or Paris inside the périphérique ≈ 10 km: avoid naming unless accurate).
