## Title
The Heaviest Spoon in the Universe

## Description
One teaspoon of neutron star weighs more than every human on Earth. Combined. A neutron star is what's left when a giant star (roughly 8–25 times the Sun's mass) dies in a supernova and its core collapses: typically about 1.4 times the Sun's mass, crushed into a ball only about 20 km across, the size of a city. Atoms are crushed so hard that protons and electrons merge into neutrons, packed at about nuclear density (on average ≈ 4 × 10¹⁷ kg per cubic metre). So a 5 mL teaspoon holds about 1–3 billion tonnes; we say "about a billion" to be conservative. All 8.2 billion humans together weigh roughly 0.4–0.5 billion tonnes. Some neutron stars spin incredibly fast: the fastest known pulsar, PSR J1748−2446ad, turns 716 times every second, and others such as PSR J0952−0607 (≈ 707 times a second) come close. And a 7 g marshmallow dropped onto one would hit at about 60% of the speed of light (≈ 180,000 km every second), releasing roughly 1–2 × 10¹⁴ J, about 20–40 kilotons of TNT: more than the Hiroshima bomb (≈ 15 kilotons).
