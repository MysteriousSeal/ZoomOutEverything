## Title
If the Sun Vanished, You Wouldn't Know for 8 Minutes

## Description
If the Sun collapsed into a black hole, you wouldn't notice for 8 minutes and 20 seconds. That's how long its last light takes to reach us, and changes in gravity travel no faster. Then the sky goes dark, but Earth keeps the same orbit, because only mass and distance matter. The whole Sun would be a black hole just 6 km (3.7 mi) wide, and within weeks the land could drop below −18 °C (0 °F), experts estimate. The twist: the Sun can never do this. It's far too light.

Crush Earth into a black hole instead. How big would it be? About the size of a marble, or about the size of a car? Tell me in the comments.

## Real answers (for reference, not for posting)
A, about the size of a marble. Earth's Schwarzschild radius is r = 2GM/c² = 2 × 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻² × 5.972 × 10²⁴ kg / (2.998 × 10⁸ m/s)² ≈ 8.87 mm, so an Earth-mass black hole would be about 1.8 cm across (a typical marble is 1.6-2.5 cm). It would still weigh as much as the whole Earth, so the Moon would keep orbiting it. B (a car, about 4.5 m) is roughly 250 times too big. For comparison, the Sun's horizon would be about 5.9 km across, as in the film.
