# 16 — What If the Sun Became a Black Hole? (90 s)

- **Series:** What If
- **Anchor:** a sunny city street at street level
- **Ladder:** the Sun collapses → for 8 min 20 s nothing changes (the light already in flight keeps arriving; changes in gravity also travel at light speed) → the last sunlight sweeps past Earth and the sky goes dark → Earth keeps exactly the same orbit, 150 million km out (only mass and distance matter) → a black hole with the Sun's mass is about 6 km wide, smaller than a city, and you could drive across it in minutes → back on Earth: within weeks the land drops below about −18 °C (estimate), the oceans freeze over from the top, and the deep water stays liquid under the ice for a very long time
- **Reveal:** the Sun can never do this. It is far too light: a star needs roughly 20 times the Sun's mass to end as a black hole. In about five billion years it will begin to swell into a red giant, then shrink to a white dwarf about the size of Earth.
- **Length:** ~90.6 s (19 lines, 214 words)
- **Accent colour:** event-horizon violet `(190, 140, 255)`. Sunlight is warm white `(255, 236, 200)`; the red giant `(255, 120, 70)`; the white dwarf `(215, 230, 255)`.
- **Music:** `deep`
- **Production:** set up on the motion engine (stubs). Sources: `BRIEF.md`, `episode.json`, `camera.js`, `env.js`, `scenes/`
- **Signature transition:** one unbroken smooth zoom-pan journey: street, then a pull-back to the Earth and Sun at true scale, where the last sunlight visibly races toward Earth and wipes across the globe. Next, a pull-back to the orbit and a 7-order-of-magnitude dive into the 6 km black hole over a city map. Then a 1 AU flight back to the frozen planet, down to the same street (now frozen and dark), out into the iced-over ocean, and a final flight back to the Sun as the black hole "un-collapses" into the real Sun, which ends as an Earth-sized white dwarf.

## Fact-check notes

- **8 min 20 s:** 1 AU = 149,597,871 km; ÷ c (299,792.458 km/s) = 499.0 s = 8 min 19 s, conventionally "about 8 min 20 s". The film uses AU = 1.496 × 10⁸ km.
- **Gravity travels at light speed:** in general relativity, changes in the gravitational field propagate at c. This was confirmed by GW170817 and GRB 170817A (2017): gravitational waves and light arrived within ~1.7 s after a 130-million-year trip, so speed(gravity) = c to about 1 part in 10¹⁵ (Abbott et al. 2017, ApJL 848 L13). Strictly, a perfectly spherical collapse would not change the outside field at all (Birkhoff's theorem), so the orbit never changes. The line says "even changes in gravity only travel at light speed", which is true either way.
- **Same orbit:** outside a spherically symmetric mass, gravity depends only on the mass and the distance (Newton's shell theorem, Birkhoff's theorem in GR). Same mass (1.989 × 10³⁰ kg) and same distance give the same orbit and the same 365-day year. The film assumes no mass is lost in the collapse.
- **150 million km:** 1 AU ≈ 149.6 million km ≈ 93.0 million mi.
- **About 6 km wide:** Schwarzschild radius r = 2GM/c² = 2 × 6.674e-11 × 1.989e30 / (2.998e8)² ≈ 2,953 m, so the horizon is ≈ 5.9 km across (3.7 mi). Its dark "shadow" would look about 2.6 × bigger (≈ 15 km) because of lensing; the label measures the horizon. Central Paris inside the Périphérique is about 11 × 9 km, so "smaller than a city" holds.
- **Drive across in minutes:** 5.9 km at 90 km/h ≈ 3.9 min (at 50 km/h, ≈ 7 min).
- **The Sun's size today:** diameter ≈ 1.39 million km (R ≈ 696,000 km). Earth's diameter is 12,742 km.
- **−18 °C within weeks (estimate):** with no sunlight, the surface radiates ~240 W/m² to space. Popular expert estimates (e.g. Laughlin, Stanford; widely quoted) put the average surface below about 0 °F / −18 °C within about a week and near −73 °C / −100 °F within a year. The oceans' heat makes the global average fall more slowly than land, so the narration says "the land", "within weeks" and "experts estimate". The on-screen label says "estimate".
- **Oceans freeze from the top, deep water stays liquid:** ice floats and insulates. Earth's internal heat (~0.09 W/m², about 47 TW) and the ocean's enormous heat content keep deep water liquid under a thickening ice lid. Estimates range from thousands to hundreds of thousands of years or more, so the narration says only "a very long time". The HUD clock stops at tens of years and makes no claim.
- **Too light, about 20× heavier:** single stars are generally thought to need an initial mass of roughly 20–25 solar masses or more to leave a black hole (lower-mass massive stars leave neutron stars; the boundary is fuzzy and depends on metallicity and rotation; Heger et al. 2003, ApJ 591 288; Sukhbold et al. 2016). The line says "about twenty times heavier". A ~20 M☉ main-sequence star has a radius of ~6–8 R☉ (drawn as 6 R☉).
- **In about five billion years, red giant:** the Sun (age 4.6 Gyr) leaves the main sequence in ~5 Gyr and reaches the red-giant tip ~7.6 Gyr from now, at ~170–256 R☉ (≈ 0.8–1.2 AU; Sackmann et al. 1993; Schröder & Smith 2008). The film draws ~0.87 AU and makes no claim about Earth's fate.
- **White dwarf about the size of Earth:** the remnant is ~0.54 M☉ with a radius of ~0.012 R☉ ≈ 8,000–9,000 km, or ~1.3 Earth radii, so "about the size of Earth" (drawn as 8,500 km beside a 6,371 km Earth disc). The future clock reads ~7.8 billion years at the white dwarf, consistent with "in about five billion years, it will **begin** to swell".
- **Pinned comment:** an isolated black hole emits no light; it would show only as a dark disc that lenses background stars. The year length is unchanged (same mass, same orbit).
