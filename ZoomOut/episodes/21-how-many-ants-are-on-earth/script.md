# 21 — How Many Ants Are on Earth? (hook format, ~77 s)

- **Series:** By the Numbers
- **Anchor:** one ant, 5 mm long, walking on bare soil
- **Ladder:** one ant → one colony under a stone (a few thousand) → a leafcutter nest (millions) → 489 ground surveys on a real globe → **20 quadrillion** (2 × 10¹⁶) → ≈ 2.5 million per person → ants outweigh all wild birds and wild mammals combined → 100+ million years (amber, dinosaurs) → lined up at 5 mm each: a line that leaves Earth, passes Neptune and Pluto, and stretches **≈ 668 AU**
- **Reveal:** the line is ~670 times the Earth–Sun distance, about 4× farther than Voyager 1 has travelled. Then back down to one ant beside a shoe: you're never really alone.
- **Length:** 76.8 s including the preroll (≈ 5.2 s) and the closing question (≈ 9.5 s); 14 lines + preroll
- **Accent colour:** amber `(235, 170, 70)` · music preset `bright`
- **Production:** set up on the motion engine (camera.js, env.js, ant.js, stub scenes). Sources: `BRIEF.md`, `episode.json`, `camera.js`, `env.js`, `ant.js`, `scenes/`
- **Signature transition:** one continuous camera over ~25 orders of magnitude. A real-anatomy ant at macro scale pulls back through a colony, a leafcutter nest and the sky to a real-coastline globe. Later the ants line up head to tail, and that column becomes a thin glowing amber line that shoots straight up off Earth, out past Neptune's and Pluto's true-scale orbits, to 668 AU, where the Sun is a dot. Then the camera falls all the way back to one ant beside a shoe.

- "best estimate" became "conservative estimate", which is the paper's own word.
- "About two and a half million" became "Nearly two and a half million", because 2.44 million is just under 2.5.

## Fact-check

- **The headline count.** Schultheiss, P. et al. (2022), "The abundance, biomass, and distribution of ants on Earth", *PNAS* 119 (40): e2201550119.
  - The paper compiles **489 studies** of ground-dwelling ants: pitfall traps and leaf-litter sampling.
  - The data cover every continent except Antarctica, which has no native ants. Coverage is uneven: tropical Africa and Asia are thin.
  - The paper's estimate is **≈ 20 × 10¹⁵ ants (20 quadrillion)**. The authors call it **conservative** because it leaves out tree-canopy and underground ants.
  - "Nearly five hundred" for 489 is fair.
- **"A two, followed by sixteen zeros."** 2 × 10¹⁶ = 20,000,000,000,000,000. That is 17 digits: a 2, then 16 zeros. ✓
- **Per person.** 2 × 10¹⁶ ÷ 8.2 × 10⁹ people (UN, 2025–26) ≈ **2.44 million**, so the script says "nearly two and a half million". The on-screen label must read "≈ 2.4 MILLION" or "NEARLY 2.5 MILLION", **not** "2.5 MILLION" bare.
- **Biomass.** Schultheiss 2022 gives ants **≈ 12 megatonnes of dry carbon (Mt C)**. Wild birds total ≈ 2 Mt C and wild mammals ≈ 7 Mt C (Bar-On, Phillips & Milo, *PNAS* 2018; Greenspoon et al., *PNAS* 2023: ≈ 3 Mt C on land + ≈ 4 Mt C in the sea). Since 12 > 2 + 7 = 9, ants outweigh both groups combined. ✓
  - Ants are also ≈ 20% of human biomass (humans ≈ 60 Mt C; 12 / 60 = 0.2). That fact is not in the narration.
  - Every visual ratio must use carbon mass: 12 : 7 : 2.
- **One colony.** Many common ants nest under stones, for example *Lasius* and *Tetramorium*. Their colonies run from a few hundred to a few tens of thousands of workers, so "a few thousand" is typical rather than a maximum.
- **Leafcutters.** Mature *Atta* colonies hold **≈ 5–8 million** workers (Hölldobler & Wilson, *The Leafcutter Ants*, 2011). Their nests reach several metres down, with hundreds of chambers. "Some leafcutter nests hold millions" ✓. *Atta texana* ranges into central Texas, which is the film's spot.
- **Deep time.** The oldest definite ant fossils are in amber about **99–100 million years old**: Kachin amber from Myanmar ≈ 99 Ma, and French Charentes amber ≈ 100 Ma. Molecular clocks put the ants' origin at ≈ 140–168 Ma. "Over a hundred million years, since the age of the dinosaurs" is honest: the fossils alone reach ~100 Ma and the lineage is older, and dinosaurs lived until 66 Ma.
  - Visual rule: no specific dinosaur may be shown that postdates the amber in a misleading way. A generic Cretaceous sauropod or theropod silhouette is fine.
- **The line.** 2 × 10¹⁶ ants × 5 mm = **1.0 × 10¹⁴ m = 100 billion km**. 1.0 × 10¹⁴ ÷ 1.496 × 10¹¹ m/AU = **668 AU**, so "about six hundred and seventy times the Earth–Sun distance" ✓.
  - 5 mm is an assumed typical worker length; workers range from ~1 mm to over 2 cm.
- **Orbits on the map** (semi-major axes in AU): Mercury 0.387, Venus 0.723, Mars 1.524, Jupiter 5.20, Saturn 9.54, Uranus 19.19, Neptune 30.07.
  - Pluto: a = 39.48, e = 0.249, so its distance from the Sun varies from 29.7 to 49.3 AU.
  - The line, drawn straight up from Earth, crosses Neptune's orbit at ≈ 30.9 AU and Pluto's at ≈ 38.0 AU.
  - Planet positions on the map are chosen for layout. Orbit radii are to scale; the planet dots are enlarged.
- **Voyager 1** is ≈ 170 AU from the Sun (late 2026; it was ~165 AU at the start of 2025 and moves ~3.6 AU per year). The line is ≈ 4× that.
- **Light travel time.** At ≈ 8.3 light-minutes per AU, light needs about 92.6 hours (≈ 3.9 days) to cover the whole line. This is for the pinned reply.
