## Title
The Year 10 Days Vanished

## Description
In October 1582, much of Catholic Europe went to bed on Thursday the 4th and woke up on Friday the 15th. Ten days, deleted by papal decree. The culprit was Julius Caesar's calendar: a leap day every four years makes an average year of 365¼ days, but Earth comes back to the spring equinox about 11 minutes sooner than that. Eleven minutes a year adds up to a whole day every 128 years, and by 1582 the calendar had slipped ten days, with Easter drifting away from the real equinox. Pope Gregory XIII deleted the days and cancelled three leap days every 400 years. That's why 1900 had no February 29 but 2000 did. Britain held out until 1752 and had to skip eleven days. And our calendar still drifts, about one day every 3,000 years.

If ten days vanished from this year, which ten would you skip? Tell me in the comments.
