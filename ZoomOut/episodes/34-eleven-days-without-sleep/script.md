## FACT-CHECK

- **Randy Gardner**, 17-year-old high-school student in San Diego, stayed awake from 28 December 1963 to 8 January 1964 (ended in 1964) for a school science fair, with friends Bruce McAllister and Joe Marciano. No stimulants (no caffeine, no drugs). (Science fair and no stimulants: Gulevich, Dement & Johnson 1966; NPR 2024, "Lessons from sleeplessness".)
- **Duration:** 264.4 hours ≈ 11 days and 24 minutes. Show "264 HOURS · 11 DAYS".
- **Observed by** Stanford sleep researcher **William Dement** (who joined for the last days) and US Navy Lt. Cmdr. John J. Ross (medical monitoring).
- **Effects** (Ross / Dement accounts; Gulevich, Dement & Johnson 1966): day 2: difficulty focusing his eyes, couldn't identify objects by touch; day 3: moodiness, coordination problems; **day 4: irritability, memory lapses, hallucinations (e.g. mistaking a street sign for a person) and the delusion that he was Paul Lowe, a famous San Diego Chargers (American football) player**; later: slurred speech, blank expression, short-term memory and concentration failures. Near the end he still beat Dement at pinball (sources disagree on day 10 or 11, so the film says "near the end").
- **Recovery:** slept about 14 hours (14 h 46 min) on the first night; no lasting damage reported at the time (he described insomnia in later life).
- **Guinness** World Records stopped accepting sleep-deprivation records because of the health risks and closed the category in 1997. ✓ Gardner's 264 h is not the record Guinness holds: it was beaten within weeks (on screen: CATEGORY CLOSED · 1997 and CASE: RANDY GARDNER, never "on file" as the record).
- **Visual notes:** no crude people: Randy as a refined ISO-style pictogram or silhouette only; a calendar/day counter (DAY 1 … DAY 11), a 264-hour clock, the eye losing focus (a blurred-then-sharp optics diagram), hands on objects (touch), a street-sign hallucination done tastefully, a football-player silhouette as a "thought", a pinball table, and a calm sleep ending. Don't claim more than the sources: no deaths, no "brain damage".
