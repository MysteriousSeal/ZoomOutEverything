## Title
11 Days Without Sleep

## Description
In 1964, a seventeen-year-old stayed awake for eleven days. No caffeine, no pills. Randy Gardner was a high-school student in San Diego, and it was a science-fair project: from 28 December 1963 to 8 January 1964 he stayed awake for 264.4 hours, about 11 days and 24 minutes. By day two his eyes couldn't focus and he couldn't identify objects by touch. By day four he was irritable and forgetful, mistook a street sign for a person, and believed he was Paul Lowe, a famous San Diego Chargers football player. By day eleven his speech slurred and his memory failed, yet near the end he still beat Stanford sleep researcher William Dement at pinball. Then he slept about 14 hours. Today, Guinness World Records no longer accepts sleep-deprivation records: it closed the category in 1997 because it's too dangerous.
