## Title
What Happens Inside Your Body in One Second?

## Description
Right now, your body is replacing almost 4 million of its own cells every second. In a single tick your heart beats about once, a nerve signal could cross a soccer field in under a second, and your bone marrow makes millions of new red blood cells. This is a continuous zoom into your own body, and the strangest number comes last.

How many times will your heart beat in a lifetime? About 3 million, or about 3 billion? Tell me in the comments.

## Real answers (for reference, not for posting)
B, about 3 billion (roughly 2.5-3 billion; our arithmetic, not a quoted figure). At a typical resting ~70 beats per minute: 70 × 60 × 24 × 365.25 ≈ 36.8 million beats a year; over ~80 years that is ≈ 2.9 billion (American Heart Association resting range 60-100 bpm; WHO life expectancy ≈ 73 years worldwide, ≈ 80 in high-income countries; 60-80 bpm over 73-80 years gives ≈ 2.3-3.4 billion). A) 3 million is only about one month of heartbeats (70 × 60 × 24 × 30 ≈ 3.0 million). Cross-check with the film: "your heart beats about once" a second, and a lifetime is about 2.5 billion seconds. The answer never appears on screen.
