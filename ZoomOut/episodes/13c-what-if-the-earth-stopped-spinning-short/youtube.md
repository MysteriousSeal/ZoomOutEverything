## Title
If Earth Stopped Spinning, a Day Would Last a Year

## Description
If the Earth stopped spinning, the winds would blow at more than 1,000 km/h (620+ mph). At the equator you are moving at about 1,670 km/h (1,040 mph) right now. Stop the planet suddenly and the air, the oceans and everything not bolted down would keep going, then the map would be redrawn. In the simplest model, a single day would last a whole year. And the strangest part: Earth really is slowing down.

At today's rate, how long until a day lasts 25 hours? Tell me in the comments.

## Real answers (for reference, not for posting)
B, about 200 million years (an approximation at today's rate). One extra hour is 3,600,000 ms. Averaged over the last ~2,700 years the day has lengthened by about 1.8 ms per century (Stephenson, Morrison & Hohenkerk 2016, Proc. R. Soc. A, https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.2016.0404; tidal braking alone gives ~2.3 ms per century, partly offset by post-glacial rebound). 3,600,000 / 1.8 ≈ 2 million centuries ≈ 200 million years. The rate is not constant (it has varied over geological time with the oceans' shape and the Moon's distance), so this is an order of magnitude at today's rate. A) 20,000 years would need ~18 seconds per century, about 10,000 times faster than measured. Cross-check with the film: ~400 million years ago a day was ~22 h.
