## Title
The Loneliest Place on Earth

## Description
There's a place in the South Pacific so remote that the closest humans are often in space. It's called Point Nemo (48°52.6′ S, 123°23.6′ W), the oceanic pole of inaccessibility. The nearest land is about 2,688 km away in three directions: Ducie Island (Pitcairn Islands) to the north, Motu Nui off Easter Island to the north-east, and Maher Island off Antarctica to the south. When the International Space Station passes overhead, its crew is only about 400 km up.

The water here sits in the South Pacific Gyre, one of the most nutrient-poor and lifeless stretches of ocean on the planet. So space agencies use it as a spacecraft cemetery: an estimated 260+ spacecraft and pieces have been brought down here since 1971, including what survived of the Russian space station Mir in 2001 (the ≈ 130-tonne station mostly burned up; its fragments fell across the area). And in the early 2030s, NASA plans to bring the ISS itself down into the same remote patch of ocean.
