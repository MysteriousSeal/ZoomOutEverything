# Zoom Out: The Loneliest Place on Earth

**Series:** True Scale · **Length:** ≈ 56.7 s · **Voice:** Brittney, Eleven v4 · **Hook:** surprising claim on frame 1 · **Ending:** comments question

## Narration ("|" = phrase pause)

0. There's a place on Earth so remote | that the closest humans are often in space.
1. It's called Point Nemo, | in the middle of the South Pacific.
2. The nearest land is almost two thousand seven hundred kilometres away, | in every direction.
3. When the space station passes overhead, | its astronauts, about four hundred kilometres up, | are your nearest neighbours.
4. The water here is some of the emptiest on the planet: | almost no nutrients, almost no life.
5. So space agencies use it as a graveyard. | Hundreds of old spacecraft lie on the seabed, | including the Russian space station Mir.
6. And around 2030, | the International Space Station is planned to join them.
7. Would you sail to Point Nemo? | Tell me in the comments.

## FACT-CHECK

- **Point Nemo** (the oceanic pole of inaccessibility): ≈ 48°52.6′S 123°23.6′W, South Pacific. Named after Jules Verne's Captain Nemo; calculated in 1992 by survey engineer Hrvoje Lukatela.
- **Nearest land:** ≈ 2,688 km to three equidistant islands: Ducie Island (Pitcairn Islands, north), Motu Nui (off Easter Island, north-east) and Maher Island (Antarctica, south). "Almost 2,700 km, in every direction" ✓. Show the three islands and the equal-distance circle on a real-coastline globe (earth.js).
- **ISS:** orbits at ≈ 400–420 km altitude; when it passes over, the crew is far closer than anyone on land (often the closest humans). "Often" ✓.
- **Empty water:** it sits in the South Pacific Gyre, one of the most nutrient-poor (oligotrophic) and lifeless ocean regions; far from land runoff, very little plankton. On screen: LOWEST CHLOROPHYLL / OF ANY OCEAN (the gyre has the lowest surface chlorophyll of the world ocean; Morel et al. 2007). Seabed depth there ≈ 3.7 km.
- **Spacecraft cemetery:** the "South Pacific Ocean Uninhabited Area" around Point Nemo is the standard target for controlled re-entries; estimates are ~260+ spacecraft and pieces since 1971 (Salyut stations, Progress cargo ships, ESA ATVs, Japanese HTVs). Mir (≈ 130 t, mostly burned up; fragments fell in the area): deorbited on 23 March 2001, only ≈ 20–25 t of fragments reached the sea, scattered over ≈ 1,500 km; shown on screen as scattered fragments, never an intact hull. "Hundreds" ✓.
- **ISS end:** NASA plans to deorbit the ISS around 2030 (operations through 2030, re-entry early 2031; "early 2030s") using the US Deorbit Vehicle, into the remote South Pacific. Say "planned" ✓.
- **Visual notes:** real-coastline globe; the ISS seen as a small accurate silhouette (no cartoon); a calm deep-blue ocean; the seabed with stylised wreck silhouettes (no gore, no fake photos). No people (astronauts can be implied by the station).
- **Sources:** Wikipedia "Pole of inaccessibility" / "Point Nemo"; NASA ISS deorbit plan (2024); ESA "Spacecraft cemetery" articles; NOAA South Pacific Gyre.
