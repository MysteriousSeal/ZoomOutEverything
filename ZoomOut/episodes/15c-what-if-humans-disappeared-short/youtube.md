## Title
Without Us, New York's Subway Floods in 2 Days

## Description
Without us, New York's subway would flood in about 2 days. Within hours the power plants would shut down, and once the pumps that keep the tunnels dry go quiet, groundwater and rain pour in. Within roughly 20 years cracked streets would start turning to forest, steel bridges would fall within a few centuries, and plastic could last a thousand years or more. But our longest-lasting trace has already left the planet: about a century of radio and TV broadcasts, now about 100 light-years out in every direction.

How many stars have our signals already reached? About 100, or over 10,000? Tell me in the comments.

## Real answers (for reference, not for posting)
B, over 10,000: roughly 10,000 to 15,000 stars lie within about 100 light-years of the Sun (an approximate count; catalogues such as Gaia's are still incomplete for the faintest nearby red dwarfs). Our earliest regular broadcasts (KDKA, 1920) are now about 106 light-years out, so the bubble has swept past all of them. "Reached" is only geometric: that far out the signals are far too weak to detect against the background noise with technology like ours. A) about 100 is roughly the number of stars within ~20 light-years.
