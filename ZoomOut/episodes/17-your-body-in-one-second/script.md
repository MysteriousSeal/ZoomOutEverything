# 17 — What Happens in Your Body in One Second? (90 s)

- **Series:** Inside You
- **Anchor:** a hand on a stopwatch (the clock starts on "Start the clock" and keeps running to the last frame)
- **Ladder:** one heartbeat (≈ 70 ml) → nerve signals up to ≈ 120 m/s (a soccer field in under a second) → ≈ 25 trillion red cells, each lapping the body in ≈ 1 minute → bone marrow makes 2–3 million new red cells every second → each lives ≈ 120 days → ≈ 0.5 L of air every few seconds → ≈ 100 W of power
- **Reveal:** you replace roughly 330 billion of your own cells every day (Sender & Milo 2021): about 1% of you, almost 4 million cells every second, mostly blood and gut. In about three months most of your blood is new. By the end of the video you're not quite the same person who started it.
- **Length:** ~89.3 s (19 lines, 215 words)
- **Accent colour:** living coral `(255, 95, 110)`; red cells a deeper blood red `(200, 30, 48)`
- **Music:** `bright`
- **Production:** in production on the motion engine. Sources: `BRIEF.md`, `episode.json`, `camera.js`, `env.js`, `scenes/`
- **Signature transition:** one continuous journey through scale. The camera dives from the hand on the stopwatch straight into the thumb's skin, into a vein among tumbling red cells, pulls out through the hand to the whole glass body, dives into the hip bone's marrow, and pulls back out to the person. Fly-through motes stream past on every dive, so the scale change feels like travel. The stopwatch keeps running throughout, and the reveal counts every cell replaced since it started.
- **Retention plan:** open loop at 6 s ("the strangest part comes last"), a new number every 4–6 s, the loop closed at 64 s ("Now, the strangest part"), and a counter that ties the reveal back to the clock started in the hook.

> Now dive into a blood vessel. | It's crowded with red blood cells.
> You have about twenty-five trillion of them, | most of the cells in your body.
> Each one laps your whole body in about a minute.
> To replace them, your bone marrow never stops.
> It makes two to three million red cells, | every single second.
> Each one lives about a hundred and twenty days, | then it's recycled.
> Zoom back out. | Every few seconds, you breathe in about half a liter of air.
> And your body runs on about a hundred watts, | like a bright old light bulb.
> Now, the strangest part.
> Every day, you replace roughly three hundred thirty billion of your own cells.
> That's about one percent of you, | almost four million cells every second.
> Mostly blood and gut. | In about three months, most of your blood is new.
> By the end of this video, | you're not quite the same person who started it.

## Fact-check notes

- **Heart rate:** a normal resting heart rate is 60–100 bpm (AHA), so the heart beats about once a second.
- **Stroke volume:**
  - About 70 ml at rest for an adult (typical range 60–100 ml; textbook value 70 ml, e.g. Guyton & Hall).
  - Imperial: 70 ml ≈ 2.4 US fl oz.
- **Nerve speed:**
  - Large myelinated Aα fibres (motor neurons and muscle-spindle afferents) conduct at about 80–120 m/s (Kandel, *Principles of Neural Science*). Touch (Aβ) fibres conduct at 35–75 m/s, hence "your **fastest** nerve signals" and "up to".
  - Imperial: 120 m/s ≈ 268 mph, shown as "≈ 270 mph".
- **Soccer field:**
  - A FIFA international pitch is 100–110 m long (Laws of the Game, typically 105 m).
  - 105 m ÷ 120 m/s = 0.875 s, so "under one second" holds.
- **Red blood cells:**
  - About 25 trillion in an adult (Sender, Fuchs & Milo 2016, *PLoS Biol*: ~25 × 10¹² RBC out of ~30 × 10¹² human cells ≈ 84%). Hence "most of the cells in your body" (by count).
- **One lap in about a minute:**
  - Blood volume ≈ 5 L and resting cardiac output ≈ 5 L/min, so the mean circulation time is ≈ 1 min.
  - Individual paths vary from ~10 s to a few minutes. "About a minute" is the average.
- **Marrow output:**
  - About 2–3 million red cells per second (≈ 2 × 10¹¹ per day). This is often cited as 2.4 million/s, and it follows from 25 × 10¹² cells ÷ (120 days × 86,400 s) ≈ 2.4 × 10⁶/s.
  - In adults, red marrow is mainly in the pelvis, vertebrae, sternum, ribs, skull and the ends of the femur and humerus. That is why the camera dives into the **hip bone**, not a finger.
- **Lifespan:**
  - About 120 days (≈ 100–120). Old cells are removed by macrophages, mostly in the spleen and liver, and their iron is recycled.
- **Breath:**
  - Resting tidal volume is about 500 ml, at 12–20 breaths per minute (one every 3–5 s). That matches "every few seconds".
  - Imperial: 0.5 L ≈ 17 US fl oz.
- **100 W:**
  - 2,000 kcal/day = 8.37 MJ ÷ 86,400 s ≈ 97 W.
  - A resting adult's basal rate is ≈ 70–90 W, and the daily average is ≈ 100 W. The narration says "runs on about a hundred watts", without "at rest", to stay honest.
  - An old incandescent bulb was 60–100 W ("a bright old light bulb").
- **Cell turnover (the reveal):**
  - Sender & Milo 2021, *Nature Medicine* 27: "The distribution of cellular turnover in the human body". They estimate ≈ 0.33 × 10¹² cells replaced per day, ≈ 1% of the body's ~30 trillion cells, and ≈ 80 g of cell mass.
  - By count, ~86% of the turnover is blood cells (mostly red cells and neutrophils) and ~12% is gut epithelium. That is the basis for "mostly blood and gut".
  - The total is a model estimate. It is narrated as "roughly" / "about".
- **Per second:**
  - 330 × 10⁹ ÷ 86,400 ≈ 3.8 × 10⁶/s, so "almost four million cells every second".
- **Three months:**
  - Red cells have a ~120-day life with a roughly uniform age spread, so after ~90 days ≈ 75% have been replaced ("most of your blood is new").
  - The gut's epithelial lining renews every ~3–5 days (Darwich et al. 2014).
- **End counter:**
  - The clock starts at ≈ 4.8 s, and the counter runs at 3.82 million/s of clock time. It reaches ≈ 320 million at the end (≈ 84.5 s × 3.82 × 10⁶). On screen it reads "≈ 320 million", and the pinned comment rounds it to "roughly 300 million".
