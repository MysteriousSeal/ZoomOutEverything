## Title
One Day of YouTube Uploads Would Take 82 Years to Watch

## Description
Every second, about 8 hours of new video land on YouTube. At 500 hours a minute, a single day of uploads is 720,000 hours: about 82 years of nonstop watching, longer than a lifetime. In that same second Google gets over 100,000 searches, and more than 99% of the traffic between continents runs under the sea, through about 1.4 million km of cable (enough to wrap the Earth 35 times), many in the deep sea as thick as a garden hose.

What damages undersea internet cables the most? Shark bites, or fishing gear and ship anchors? Tell me in the comments.

## Real answers (for reference, not for posting)
B, fishing gear and ship anchors, by far. The International Cable Protection Committee (ICPC) says approximately 70-80% of cable incidents come from accidental damage by commercial fishing and ship anchors (other ICPC-based tallies: 65-75%; TeleGeography: "two-thirds of all cable faults"); dragged anchors alone cause about 30% of incidents, around 60 faults a year, out of roughly 150-200 faults worldwide each year. A) Shark and fish bites caused well under 1% of faults historically (about 0.5% in 1959-2006) and none were recorded from 2007 to at least 2014 (ICPC data, via Fibre Systems). Natural hazards (earthquakes, underwater landslides, currents) account for about 10%, component failures about 5%. The answer never appears in the film.
