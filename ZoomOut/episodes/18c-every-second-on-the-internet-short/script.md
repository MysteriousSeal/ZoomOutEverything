# 18 — Every Second on the Internet (90 s)

- **Series:** By the Numbers
- **Anchor:** one phone lighting up on a nightstand in the dark (a Midtown Manhattan apartment)
- **Ladder:** 100,000+ Google searches a second (8 billion+ a day) → ~4 million emails a second (nearly half spam) → ~8 hours of new YouTube video a second (500+ hours a minute) → data centres use ~1.5% of the world's electricity (≈ France) → the data crosses oceans through ~1.4 million km of undersea cable (≈ 35 times around the Earth, many as thin as a garden hose, 99%+ of intercontinental traffic)
- **Reveal:** at 500 hours a minute, a single day of YouTube uploads (720,000 hours) would take about **82 years** to watch nonstop. Longer than a lifetime.

> What happens on the internet in one single second?

> All of it has to live somewhere: | in vast warehouses of computers called data centers.
> Experts estimate they use about one and a half percent of all the world's electricity.
> Roughly as much as the whole of France.
> And to cross from one continent to another, | the data doesn't fly through space.
> It goes under the sea.
> About one point four million kilometers of cable lie on the ocean floor.
> That's enough to wrap around the Earth thirty-five times.
> In the deep sea, | many are about as thick as a garden hose.
> Yet they carry more than ninety-nine percent of the traffic between continents.
> Now, back to that one phone, | and those eight hours of YouTube every second.
> One single day of uploads | would take about eighty-two years to watch, nonstop. | Longer than a lifetime.

## Fact-check notes

- **Google searches:** Google said in March 2025 it handles "more than 5 trillion searches a year" ≈ 13.7 billion/day ≈ 158,000/s. The long-cited older estimate is ~8.5 billion/day ≈ 99,000/s (Internet Live Stats). "More than a hundred thousand a second" and "over eight billion a day" are true under both. The HUD counter uses a conservative 100,000/s (≈ 9 million over 90 s, matching the pinned comment).
- **Emails:** Radicati Group / Statista: ~361 billion emails/day in 2024 (forecast ~376 billion for 2025). 361e9 / 86,400 ≈ 4.2 million/s, so "about four million".
- **Spam:** Statista / Kaspersky put spam at ~45–49% of global email traffic in 2023–2024, so "nearly half". On screen: ≈ 45%.
- **YouTube uploads:** "500+ hours of video uploaded every minute" (YouTube, 2019, still the figure YouTube and press use; likely higher today). 500 / 60 = 8.33 h/s, so "about eight hours".
- **Reveal maths:** 500 h/min × 1,440 min = 720,000 h/day. 720,000 / 8,766 h per year (365.25 days) = **82.1 years**. "Longer than a lifetime": global life expectancy at birth is ~73 years (UN WPP 2024). Because the 500 h/min figure is from 2019, today's true number is probably higher, so 82 is a floor.
- **Data-centre electricity:** IEA *Energy and AI* (April 2025): data centres used ~415 TWh in 2024, about 1.5% of global electricity consumption. The narration says "experts estimate" and "about".
- **France comparison:** France's annual electricity consumption is ~440–450 TWh (RTE *Bilan électrique 2024*: ~449 TWh in 2024, weather-corrected, incl. losses; https://assets.rte-france.com/prod/public/2025-09/04092025-Bilan-electrique-2024-rapport-complet.pdf). 415 vs ~449 TWh (−8%), so "roughly as much as the whole of France". Note: the s10 label's small line "about 415 TWh a year" is the data-centre figure, not France's.
- **Undersea cables, length:** TeleGeography: "nearly 1.4 million kilometers of submarine cables in service globally" (early 2023 and early 2024 figures, "Submarine Cable 101"). Its FAQ now says "As of early 2026, we believe there are over 1.5 million kilometers" (https://www2.telegeography.com/submarine-cable-faqs-frequently-asked-questions). The narration's "about 1.4 million" (and 35×) is the 2024 figure and is now a conservative floor (in-service cable only; retired cable also still lies on the sea floor). Updating to "1.5 million / 37 times" would need a re-voice plus new s13/s14 labels and rings, so it was left as is for this cut. On screen in imperial: 1.4 million km ≈ 870,000 mi.
- **35 times around:** 1,400,000 km / 40,075 km (equatorial circumference) = 34.9, so "thirty-five times".
- **Garden hose:** Deep-ocean lightweight cable is ~17–21 mm in diameter (TeleGeography FAQ: "For most of its journey across the ocean, a cable is typically as wide as a garden hose"). Cable near shore is armoured and much thicker, hence "in the deep sea, many are…". On screen: ≈ 2 cm / 0.8 in.
- **99%:** "Submarine cables account for over 99% of intercontinental data traffic": TeleGeography checked this claim and rates it true (https://resources.telegeography.com/2023-mythbusting-part-3), based on FCC data that satellites carried 0.37% of US international capacity (cited in TeleGeography's FAQ). Satellites carry well under 1%.
- **"Doesn't fly through space":** shorthand for "doesn't go via satellites". True for >99% of intercontinental traffic.
- **Visual honesty:** the cable map (`geo.js`) is a stylised subset (~36 corridors) of real systems on their real sea routes. The data-centre campus sits where New York's big interconnection campuses are (Secaucus, NJ). The globe uses real NOAA ETOPO1 elevation data. City lights are a stylised model sized by metro population.
