## Fact-check notes

- **Framing:** every timescale is an expert estimate or projection (the scenario cannot be tested). Main source: Alan Weisman, *The World Without Us* (2007), plus engineers interviewed there and in similar documentaries (e.g. *Life After People*, 2008). Narration uses "would", "about", "might", "perhaps".
- **Power plants within hours:** fossil plants need continuous fuel delivery, operators and load balancing; without them, automatic safety trips and grid instability cascade into blackouts within hours (Weisman; *Life After People*). Some hydro and solar output could last longer, hence "power plants would shut down" rather than "every light".
- **Subway in about 2 days:** NYC Transit pumps about 13 million US gallons (≈ 49 million litres: 13e6 × 3.785 L) of groundwater and rain out of the subway every day (MTA). In Weisman's book, transit engineers estimate that with the pumps off, the system would flood in about 36 hours, so "about two days" is a rounded, honest figure. On screen: "≈ 50 million litres a day · 13 million US gal".
- **About 20 years, streets to forest:** freeze-thaw cycles crack asphalt within a few winters; weeds and fast-growing trees (e.g. *Ailanthus*) root in the cracks; Weisman describes Manhattan becoming a young forest within roughly two decades.
- **A few centuries, steel bridges and towers fall:** unpainted steel corrodes; Weisman's engineers expect most of the city's bridges to fall within about 300 years, and steel-framed buildings to fail over a similar span as their foundations corrode. (Some masonry and the Hell Gate Bridge could last longer.)
- **Plastic 1,000+ years:** published decomposition estimates for common plastics range from decades to roughly a thousand years, and some polymers may persist far longer (no direct measurements; NOAA/agency figures are estimates). Hence "could… perhaps a thousand years or more".
- **Bronze millions of years:** Weisman reports that bronze sculptures, being corrosion-resistant, could keep a recognisable shape for millions of years, among the longest-lasting human artefacts. Speculative, hence "might".
- **Radio bubble:** regular broadcast radio began around 1920 (KDKA, Pittsburgh); TV from the late 1930s. Radio waves travel at the speed of light (299,792 km/s), so the earliest broadcasts are now about 2026 − 1920 ≈ 106 light-years from Earth: **a bubble about 100 light-years in radius (about 200 light-years across)**. The producer's "about 100 light-years wide" is stated as a radius ("about a hundred light-years in every direction"), which is the correct reading. Caveat for replies: the signals fade with distance (inverse square), and beyond a few light-years most would be undetectable against the noise with today's technology. "Trace" (not "message") is chosen for that reason.
- **Light-year:** 9.46 × 10¹⁵ m. About 100 ly ≈ 9.5 × 10¹⁷ m. Roughly 10,000–15,000 stars lie within 100 ly of the Sun.
- **Pinned comment:** the bubble already contains thousands of stars; any reply would take as long again to return.
