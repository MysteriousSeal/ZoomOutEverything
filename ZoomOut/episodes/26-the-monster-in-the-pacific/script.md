## FACT-CHECK (every number on screen must match this)

- **Now:** NOAA CPC's 8 Oct 2026 discussion: Niño-3.4 +2.1 °C, Niño-3 +3.0 °C, Niño-1+2 +3.9 °C (weekly anomalies). IRI (19 Oct 2026): traditional Niño-3.4 weekly value centred on 14 Oct 2026 = **+3.3 °C**; Sept monthly +2.84 °C. "Parts of the Pacific are three degrees hotter than normal" is safe. On screen: "+3.3 °C · NIÑO 3.4 · WEEK OF 14 OCT 2026" (IRI) and/or "+3.9 °C OFF PERU" (Niño-1+2, NOAA).
- **Forecast strength:** Carbon Brief (2026): of 667 model runs from 14 groups, **more than 91%** predict the strongest El Niño in the modern record. The 2015–16 record peaked around **2.75 °C**; the median forecast peak is about **3.6 °C**. IRI plume ≈ +3.5 °C for Oct–Dec and Nov–Jan. Peak expected around December 2026.
- **The record year:** WMO (May 2026): El Niño "increases the chances of the following year, 2027, being the next record-breaking year". Met Office (Aug 2026): 2027 "very likely" to replace 2024 as the warmest year. Zeke Hausfather (Aug 2026): **≈ 95% chance** 2027 is the hottest on record, central estimate **≈ 1.76 °C** above pre-industrial (middle range 1.67–1.85 °C). Current record: **2024, ≈ 1.55 °C** (WMO). Say "could" / "forecast", never "will".
- **What El Niño is:** a periodic warming of the central and eastern tropical Pacific, as the trade winds weaken and warm water that normally piles up in the west spreads east along the equator towards South America. It typically peaks around Northern-Hemisphere winter.
- **Impacts (typical, high-confidence El Niño patterns):** drought in Australia, Indonesia and the Amazon / northern South America; heavy rain and floods in Peru and Ecuador, the southern United States, and East Africa (Oct–Dec short rains). Say "can bring" / "typically", not "will". Don't name 2026 disasters that aren't confirmed.
