## Title
The Monster in the Pacific

## Description
Something is waking up in the Pacific. It's El Niño: when the trade winds weaken, a giant pool of warm water spreads east across the ocean. Right now, parts of the Pacific are more than three degrees above normal (Niño 3.4 weekly reading: +3.3 °C, week of 14 October 2026), in a warm stripe more than 10,000 km long. Over 91% of model runs say it will become the strongest El Niño on record, stronger than the "Godzilla" El Niño of 2015–16. El Niño moves rain around the planet: drought in Australia and the Amazon, floods in Peru, Ecuador, the southern US and East Africa. Crops can fail and food prices can climb. And on top of global warming, it could make 2027 the hottest year ever recorded: climate scientist Zeke Hausfather puts the odds at about 95%.
