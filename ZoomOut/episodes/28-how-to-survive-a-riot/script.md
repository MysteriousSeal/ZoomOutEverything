## FACT-CHECK

There is no single official government guide for riots; the advice combines crowd-safety science and journalist-safety guidance. Say "often", never claim statistics we don't have.

- **The crowd is the danger:** in crowd disasters most deaths are from compressive asphyxia (people crushed so they can't breathe), not trampling or violence. Crowd-science professor G. Keith Still: people "don't die because they panic, they panic because they're dying". Dangerous density starts around 5+ people per m²; at 6–7 per m² a crowd starts to flow like a fluid and surges (shock waves) can push people off their feet.
- **Leave early / don't film:** journalist-safety guidance (RSF, others): avoid the area, leave as soon as the mood shifts; walk, don't run (running draws attention); bystanders who stop to watch or film get caught up.
- **Edges and pinch points:** crushes happen at bottlenecks and against obstacles: walls, fences, barriers, pillars, narrow streets, tunnels and stairs. Stay at the edges of the crowd, near an exit route, away from those.
- **Surges:** don't push against a surge; go with it and, in the pause between waves, move sideways and diagonally towards the edge (zig-zag out), as crowd-safety experts describe.
- **Arms up like a boxer:** fold your arms in front of your chest (boxer's stance) to keep a little space to breathe and stay balanced.
- **If you fall:** get up immediately; if you can't, curl into a ball on your side (ideally against a wall or obstacle), protect your head with your arms.
- **Tear gas (CS):** move to fresh air, upwind; CS is heavier than air, so it pools low: go uphill/higher ground; don't rub eyes; flush eyes and skin with plenty of clean water; take out contact lenses; change and wash clothes before going inside where possible.
- **Indoors:** get off the street into a building, away from windows (projectiles, broken glass).
- Avoid: weapons, fighting tips, police-confrontation advice, naming real events or real cities, showing violence.
- **Sources:** Reporters Without Borders (RSF) safety guide for covering civil unrest; G. Keith Still / crowd-science interviews (Slate, Wikipedia "Crowd collapses and crushes"); journalist-safety and travel-security guides.
