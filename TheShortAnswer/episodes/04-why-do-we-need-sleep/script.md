# 04 — Why Do We Need Sleep?

- **Category:** Body & health
- **Length:** ~27s (69 words)
- **Core idea:** Sleep is active maintenance: the brain cycles through stages, flushes out waste, and consolidates memory, and skipping it impairs you measurably.
- **Hook:** A silhouette asleep in a dark room, a clock spinning through the night: "Why do we spend a third of our lives unconscious?"

## Narration script

> Why do we spend a third of our lives unconscious?
> While you sleep, your brain is anything but off.
> It cycles through stages, roughly every ninety minutes.
> In deep sleep, fluid pulses through the brain and flushes out waste.
> Along the way, it replays the day and files away what matters.
> Skip it, and your reactions slow down almost like you've been drinking.
> Sleep isn't wasted time. It's maintenance.

## Fact-check notes

- **A third of life:** ~8 h of 24. Over a ~79-year life that's ≈ 26 years.
- **Cycles:** NREM/REM cycles last roughly 90 minutes (range ~70–120), with about 4–6 per night.
- **Waste clearance:** the glymphatic system clears metabolites (e.g. beta-amyloid) faster during sleep (Xie et al., 2013, in mice). In humans, slow waves of cerebrospinal fluid pulse through the brain during deep NREM sleep (Fultz et al., 2019). This research is still active, so keep the claim modest.
- **Memory:** memories are replayed and consolidated during sleep, mostly in deep NREM (hippocampal replay), with REM also involved. That's why the script doesn't tie replay to REM.
- **Drunk comparison:** after 17–19 hours awake, performance on some tests is comparable to a blood alcohol level of 0.05% (Williamson & Feyer, 2000).
- Educational only, not medical advice.
