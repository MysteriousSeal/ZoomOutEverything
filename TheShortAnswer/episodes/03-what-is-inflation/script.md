# 03 — What Is Inflation?

- **Category:** Money & economy
- **Length:** ~28s (72 words)
- **Core idea:** When money grows faster than the stuff it can buy, each unit of money buys less, and slowly compounding prices quietly halve your savings.
- **Hook:** A price counter rising year by year: "Why does everything cost more every year?"
- **Accent colour:** amber `(240, 190, 90)`
- **Signature transition:** the price counter's digits melt and pour into the next scene's shelves of goods; at the end the cash stack dissolves into particles that drift back up into the hook's price counter (loop)
- **Production:** not rendered yet

## Narration script

> Why does everything cost more every year?
> Picture an economy with a fixed amount of stuff to buy.
> Now the amount of money grows faster than the stuff does.
> More money chasing the same goods means sellers can raise prices.
> That's inflation: every dollar quietly buys a little less.
> At just two percent a year, prices double in about thirty-five years.
> So cash sitting in a drawer is slowly melting.

## Fact-check notes

- The core mechanism is **demand-pull inflation** (more money and spending chasing the same goods). There's also **cost-push** inflation (e.g. energy or wages getting pricier), and expectations play a role. The script simplifies to the money-vs-goods story.
- "Money grows faster than the stuff" is a simplified quantity-theory framing. In the real world, how fast money changes hands and how much output grows matter too.
- **Rule of 70:** doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ rate → 70 ÷ 2 = 35 years (exact: ln 2 / ln 1.02 ≈ 35.0).
- After 35 years at 2%, $10,000 in cash buys what ≈ $5,000 buys today.
- The Fed and the ECB both target about **2%** inflation per year.
- The $1.00 → $1.50 tags are illustrative, not real data.
